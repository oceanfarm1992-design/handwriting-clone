Handwriting Clone

Turn a Word, PDF or text document into pages written in your own handwriting. Everything runs in your browser, so your handwriting never leaves your device.

Start by picking a sample handwriting, or capture your own from a photo, on screen or with the template.

1Capture your handwriting

Three ways to capture it. You can mix them and add more versions of a letter at any time.

From a photo of your writing no template

Photograph a page of notes you already wrote, or copy a short practice paragraph onto any paper. Tell the app what the page says and it finds your letters, and your spacing, by itself.

Write on screen

Use a finger, stylus or mouse on your phone, tablet or computer. Takes about 5 minutes for all 82 characters.

Or print, write with a pen and scan

  1. Print the template (two pages, A4). Pages A and B give each letter two different shapes.
  2. Write one character in every box with a dark pen, resting it on the dashed line.
  3. Scan or photograph each page flat, in good light, with all four black corner squares visible, then upload the images.

Or pick a sample handwriting

Ready-made styles for trying the app, or for when you would rather not use your own. These are open handwriting fonts, not real people's writing. All except the first are fetched from Google Fonts when chosen.

Formatting you can use
  • # Title gives a heading with a pen underline. Lines like Meaning :- or Main points: are underlined too.
  • - point, 1. step or a) item make lists; long items wrap neatly under the text.
  • __key words__ underlines words. ~~word~~ scribbles a word out. ==key point== highlights it with a marker.
  • | Name | Marks | makes a table row. Tables from Word and PDF files come in like this.

Learn from a photo

  1. Copy the practice paragraph below onto any paper in your normal handwriting. Or use a page you already wrote and type what it says instead.
  2. Photograph the whole page straight on, in good light.
  3. Choose the photo. The text below must match the page word for word, including spelling mistakes.

Works best when most letters are written separately and words have clear gaps. Fully joined cursive cannot be read this way.

Write on screen

Small letters sit between the two lower lines. Tall letters reach the top dashed line. Tails of g, p and y go below the solid baseline.
